As the owner,
I want to pick a change I made and see, per metric and per band, how the stories before it compare with the stories after it, with the verdict in words and the evidence under it,
So that "is it developing faster, slower, or no change, and how has the cost been impacted" has an honest answer even with a handful of stories (addendum §3.2 F-J; research §7).

**Acceptance Criteria:**

**Given** `GET /analytics/compare` and `GET /analytics/changes` (Story 40.2) and the chart kit (Story 40.3)
**When** `/app/analytics/compare?change=<id>` is opened (from a change mark, from the changes list, or from the settings page of Story 40.6)
**Then** the change is named in words at the top ("You changed the dev leg's model from claude-sonnet-5 to claude-opus-5-5 on 2026-11-12 at 09:14"), a selector offers every change and the route form ("stories whose dev stage ran on A against B"), and for each metric (cost at list price, cycle time, hands-on time, the changed leg's stage minutes, review passes, host-leg minutes) a row shows a dumbbell of the two medians with the ranges as whiskers and n printed on each end, the verdict word with its text, and the two sides' points as strip plots under it; the band selector defaults to the rows per band with the pooled row shown only when the API says the mixes match; the caveats read in full under the verdicts; "Not enough stories yet: 2 of 3 on the new route" shows the points with no dumbbell; hover and focus give the story and its value; the table twin lists both sides; `src/analytics/compare.test.ts` pins the words for each verdict, the dumbbell's ends, the strip plots and the caveat rendering from fixtures.

**Given** the owner's rule of 2026-11-05 and the research's warning on perception (§7 point 7)
**When** a verdict is shown
**Then** the verdict text always carries both counts, both medians and both ranges, never a bare word; the page never shows a mean or a trend line through fewer than five points; a change with no landed story on either side reads "No stories have run on this route yet"; the definitions drawer explains the rule in one paragraph ("Three stories a side to say anything; faster or slower when the medians differ by more than a fifth and the middle halves do not overlap; the same band on both sides"); `src/analytics/compare.test.ts` pins the rule text; `apps/studio-web/README.md` records the story.
