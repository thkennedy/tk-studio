## Intent
The office still draws from 30.1's placeholder sheet, which 30.4 extended to a desk and eight shared state sets. This story replaces it with our own sprite sheet (D6). The source files live in `apps/studio-web/assets/`, and a script in `tools/sprites/` exports `public/sprites/office.png` plus a JSON atlas, byte-reproducibly. The renderer builds its frame sets from the atlas by name, per role. A missing (role, state) pose falls back to the role's `idle` with a console warning. The placeholder sheet and its script are deleted, and `kb/studio-web-assets.md` records the tool, the pipeline, the format and the credits. The supervisor and the contract are untouched (web-only, AD-2).

## Acceptance Criteria
- AC1: **Given** the art tools on the PC (Adobe Creative Cloud or a tool with agentic or MCP support, the operator's choice named in the record) **When** the pipeline is built **Then** `apps/studio-web/assets/` holds the source files and `tools/sprites/` a script that exports the committed sheet `apps/studio-web/public/sprites/office.png` plus its JSON atlas (frame names `<role>.<state>.<n>`, 16 by 16 or 32 by 32 tiles, nearest-friendly, no anti-aliased edges), the export is reproducible (the same source gives the same bytes, checked by a test), and the sheet carries the floor, one desk, and every role of the registry in every office state of §7.3 with at least two frames per looping state

## Settled shape
These are the planner's rulings on shapes the design documents constrain. They are not appended to SPEC.md, because the gate session's write surface excludes it (the 30-4 and 30-5 plans' precedent).
- **The atlas is Pixi v8's spritesheet JSON (hash form)**, at `apps/studio-web/public/sprites/office.json`:
  - `{"frames": {"<name>": {"frame": {"x","y","w","h"}, "rotated": false, "trimmed": false, "spriteSourceSize": {"x":0,"y":0,"w","h"}, "sourceSize": {"w","h"}}}, "meta": {"image": "office.png", "format": "RGBA8888", "size": {"w","h"}, "scale": "1"}}`.
  - Keys are sorted, written with fixed separators and a trailing newline, so it is byte-stable.
  - The names:
    - `floor.0` and `desk.0`;
    - `<role>.<state>.<n>`, with `n` from 0 and contiguous.
  - 16 by 16 tiles (`TILE` stays 16, and `pickZoom` is unchanged).
- **Looping states.** All eight §7.3 states loop: `placeDesks` loops every pose, and the placeholder gives each state two frames. So every role carries at least two frames in every state, 7 × 8 × ≥2 = at least 112 character frames.
- **The png.** RGBA8 with no ancillary chunks (no `tIME`, `gAMA` or text). Every scanline uses filter 0. The deflate level is fixed. Every pixel's alpha is 0 or 255, which is what "no anti-aliased edges" means here. The frame order is deterministic: floor, desk, then `ROLES` × `OFFICE_STATES` × n.
- **The `Sheet` from the atlas.**
  - `Sheet` gains `roles: Record<role, Partial<Record<OfficeState, Texture[]>>>` (it already satisfies `FrameSheet`).
  - `frames` (the shared set for a role not in the sheet) is the first role in `ROLES` order that has the state.
  - `character` (the spike's lone sprite in `layoutScene`) is that role's `idle.0`.
  - A pure `sheetFromAtlas(textures: Record<string, Texture>)` replaces `cutSheet` so tests drive it without WebGL. A name that does not parse is ignored with a warning.

## Risks and open decisions
- **Block If: the art tool is the operator's ruling (SPEC Open Question 1).**
  - AC1 says the tool is "the operator's choice named in the record", and the design spec's D6 leaves it open: Adobe CC, or a tool with agentic or MCP support. A headless session cannot drive Adobe CC.
  - Halt `blocked` unless a `ruling:` commit or the replan queue's "operator — from 30-6-the-status-screen-and-the-home" entry records one of these:
    - **(a)** Tool-neutral, loop-authored. The sources are text pixel grids (a palette plus character rows per frame, as `make-placeholder.ts` writes them) under `apps/studio-web/assets/`, the tool of record is "the loop, via `tools/sprites/export.py`", and the operator may redraw later in any tool that writes the same format.
    - **(b)** An attended tool (Adobe CC, Aseprite or another). The operator authors the sources, for example layered PNGs or one PNG per role, before the story runs. The exporter packs those exports, and the session never edits art.
    - **(c)** An agentic or MCP tool the operator names, available in the engine sandbox.
  - The Files list above holds for (a), and for (b) when the sources are PNGs. Any other source format means the exporter must parse it, which is a deviation that needs the ruling's words.
- **oversize.** There are seventeen Files entries, the assets folder included, against L's "larger, with a stated reason". The reason: the pipeline (sources, exporter, test, outputs), the renderer swap and the retirement all land together, because AC2 says the placeholder is deleted when the sheet lands. Most entries are small (deletions, a comment, a pointer line). Halt `blocked` if the Files list grows past these.
- **Art volume.** At least 112 character frames plus the floor and the desk. Under (a), keep one body per role with per-state overlays (marker, pose pixel) generated from a shared base, so the source stays reviewable. Never import third-party pixels or reference packs (D6: no LimeZu, no share-alike).
- **Reproducibility across hosts.** zlib output can differ between zlib builds at a level. The test compares a fresh export with the committed bytes, so pin the level and the strategy and use Python's bundled zlib. If the host's `verify_host.py` and the sandbox disagree on bytes, record it and halt: never loosen the test to a pixel comparison without a ruling.
- **No supervisor or contract change.** The atlas is a static web asset. Personas stay as they are (`personas.json` display names; sprites per persona are later work).
- **The sandbox refuses symlinks**, so verify bun in a /tmp copy. The host leg is binding. No new web dependency is expected; if one is added, regenerate `bun.lock`.
- **unverifiable-here.** The attended look of the new office on the desktop and the iPad (crisp edges at ratio 1, 2 and 3, every role's loops), and the operator's confirmation that the record names their tool.
